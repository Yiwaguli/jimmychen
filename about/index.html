About

Jimmy Chen is an artist and PhD researcher at the Creative Computing Institute, University of the Arts London, working across posthumanism, queer theory and multi-sensory HCI.

His practice moves between film, speculative worldbuilding, virtual reality, 3D prototyping, scent, sound and live performance. Across these media it keeps returning to the senses that screens tend to leave out: smell, taste, touch and the felt weight of things. Earlier works used food, pheromones and edible objects to imagine how bodies might communicate beyond words. More recent work turns towards fungi, robotics and contemporary ritual.

His doctoral research, ‘Queering with Fungi’, explores multispecies relations through auto-ethnography and critical biodata interaction. Through embodied and non-representational methodologies, his practice challenges normative paradigms, disrupting extractivist technologies and advocating for radical reattunement.

His work has been selected for Ars Electronica (2026) and Being Human Festival (2026), and presented at several academic conferences. He holds an MA in Digital Direction from the Royal College of Art and teaches as an Associate Lecturer at the Creative Computing Institute.
